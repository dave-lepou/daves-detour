The Power of (EQ) Emotional Intelligence

In this blog, I'm sharing my understanding of emotional intellegence. I'll talk about what it is, how it differs from IQ and why it matters to me.

What is Emotional Intelligence?

Emotional Intelligence (EQ) is the ability to recognise, understand, and manage our own emotions while empathising with the emotions and perspectives of others to guide our actions. High or low EQ depends on our level of self-awareness, self-regulation, situational awareness, and empathy. For example, if my friend 'Billy' makes a cup of tea by pouring milk before the hot water, a low-EQ response would be to immediately criticise him, imposing a "my way is right" mindset that ignores his background and habits. A high-EQ response involves exercising self-regulation to pause the impulse to judge - showing curiosity about his approach and recognising that there are many valid ways to reach the shared goal of making a cup of tea.

How is EQ different from IQ?

While IQ measures cognitive ability, logical reasoning, and technical problem-solving, EQ measures how effectively we understand, manage, and navigate our emotions and relationships. Using the cup of tea analogy, an "IQ lens" focuses on procedural knowledge and execution - knowing that water must reach boiling point, understanding the function of tea bags and following logical steps to prepare the cup of tea. In contrast, an "EQ lens" focuses on the interpersonal environment - showing awareness of how people prefer their tea, managing your reaction if someone brews it differently and maintaining a positive connection while achieving the goal. In short, IQ helps you solve the problem, while EQ helps you manage the people and context surrounding it.

Questions that came up during my research (and answers I found)

1. How deliberate can you be when assigning values? Does spacing have to be even on all sides?

Not at all! You can assign custom values to each individual side. One way I learned is using CSS shorthand. Instead of writing four separate rules for top, right, bottom and left, you can write:

margin: 0px (=TOP) 5px (=RIGHT) 10px (=BOTTOM) 15px (=LEFT);

The ways I learned to remember how the browser reads this are to think of a clock and work clockwise from Top-Right-Bottom-Left; or remember the acronym TRBL (read it as the word "TROUBLE")

2. Do borders work the exact same way as margins and padding?

Not quite. Borders require a bit more detail because you need to specify three properties for the line to display correctly: size, style, colour. For e.g. if you only require a line along the bottom of an element, you would write: border-bottom: 10px solid white;

3. Are borders always rectangular and solid?

No. There are different shapes you can use as borders. For e.g if you required a rounded border you would use the CSS rule: border-radius: 10px;

There are also different styled borders you can use. For e.g there is a dashed border; double border; and a few more. This feature enhances your ability to personalise each element further and style your page as you wish!

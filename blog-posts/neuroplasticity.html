Rewiring for Growth: How Neuroplasticity shapes the way we learn!

In this blog, i'll introduce the concepts of neuroplasticity and growth mindset; explain how they can benefit people like me who are starting a new learning journey; and share some practical strategies you can adopt to support your own learning and growth.

What are these concepts?

Neuroplsticity - The "Running Shoe" analogy

Think of neuroplasticity like breaking in a new pair of running shoes. The first time you wear them, they feel stiff and uncomfortable - causing blisters and hot spots because your feet aren't used to them.

With repetitive use, the shoe physically moulds to the unique shape of your foot. Eventually, running becomes effortless because the shoe feels like an extension of your body.

Neuroplasticity works the exact same way! practicing a new mental skill or emotional response feels awkward and difficult at first. But with repetition, your brain physically adapts its neural connections to fit that new habit until it becomes completely natural.

Growth-Mindset - "The Corridor" vs. "The House"

To truly understand a growth mindset, I first had to recognise how often I defaulted to a fixed mindset - a state I call "staying in the corridor."

The corridor is a narrow, rigid space. When you are stuck in a fixed mindset, you fixate entirely on the single exit at the end of the hall. While reaching the end is a goal, this tunnel vision blinds you to the closed doors along the walls. Inside those rooms lie better resources, smarter tools, and creative shortcuts you ignore because you are set in "one way" of doing things.

A growth mindset means viewing the entire house, not just the corridor. It invites change by encouraging you to open those side doors. It requires you to be open, adaptable, and optimistic - realising that exploring new rooms ultimately enhances your decision-making and leads to far greater results.

How can these benefit someone on a new learning journey?

Starting a new learning journey requires appreciating these concepts, because they remind us that feeling "dumb" today doesn't mean you're incapable. Your brain just needs time to solidify new neural pathways and to make that happen, you have to do your part by remaining open to practice, repetition, mistakes, and feedback!

Respecting this process creates a shift in perspective: the struggle you feel at the start is part of the process, NOT evidence of failure. Initial discomfort is only temporary and your current ability doesn't define your future capability.

Allowing these principles to build your resilience changes how you learn. Instead of avoiding uncomfortable challenges out of fear, resilience empowers you to tackle difficult problems head-on, always keeping you open to continuous growth.
